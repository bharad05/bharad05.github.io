About

I am a PhD researcher and Graduate Student Researcher (GSR) in Civil and Environmental Engineering at UC Davis, advised by Prof. Alvar Escriva-Bou. My broader research lies at the intersection of environmental economics, climate finance, and water resources systems engineering. My dissertation focuses on drought risk and explores how economic and financial tools can strengthen the resilience of water systems and support climate adaptation in an increasingly uncertain future.

My research explores how hydrologic uncertainty evolves into institutional, financial, and policy risk in water-scarce regions such as California. I integrate water resources modeling, climate science, environmental economics, and risk analysis to translate complex datasets into actionable insights for drought planning and climate adaptation. More broadly, I am interested in developing decision-support frameworks, financial instruments, and market-based approaches that enable governments, utilities, and communities to manage drought proactively rather than simply respond to its impacts.

Experiences across research, engineering, and international development have shaped my interest in developing practical solutions to water-related risks. I earned my Bachelor's degree in Civil Engineering from Jawaharlal Nehru Technological University Hyderabad (JNTUH) before working with the Morobe Development Foundation on United Nations-supported watershed and environmental management initiatives in Papua New Guinea. I later completed my Master's degree at University College London (UCL), where I continued as a Graduate Student Researcher, developing optimization models for water quality trading while evaluating their economic and policy implications for farming communities in the United Kingdom. During this time, I also contributed to research on sustainable housing and urban development in Greater London. These experiences reinforced my belief that addressing water challenges requires integrating engineering with economics, finance, and policy. My long-term goal is to develop practical, evidence-based solutions that make drought risk more measurable, water systems more resilient, and climate adaptation more effective for the communities and institutions that depend on them.

These projects connect engineering, economics, climate data, and institutional decision-making to address water-related risks across California, the United Kingdom, India, and Papua New Guinea.
